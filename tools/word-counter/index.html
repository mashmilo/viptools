Word Counter

Free word counter. Count words, characters, sentences, paragraphs and reading time as you type. Runs in your browser, nothing uploaded.

Word Counter is a free online tool that measures the length of any text in real time. It reports words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs and estimated reading time, all without uploading your writing anywhere. Everything runs inside your browser, so your drafts stay private.

How to use the Word Counter

Paste or type your text into the box above. The numbers update with every keystroke, so you never need to press a button. To check a single paragraph, paste only that paragraph. To check a full essay, paste the entire document. Clear the box to start again. The reading time estimate assumes an average adult pace of about 238 words per minute, which is a common benchmark used by publishers.

Why word count matters

Length is a hidden requirement in almost every kind of writing. Students must meet essay limits, and a few words over can cost marks. Freelancers are paid per word and need accurate totals before invoicing. Social platforms cap posts, and search engines display only the first 150 to 160 characters of a meta description, so counting characters helps you write snippets that are not cut off. Bloggers also use length as a planning tool: in-depth guides that cover a topic thoroughly tend to earn more links and rankings than thin pages, and a counter shows whether your article is substantial enough.

Tips for better writing with word counts

Set a target before you write. A product description may need 100 words, a news post 400, and a tutorial 1,200 or more. Write the first draft freely, then use the counter to trim filler. Short sentences improve readability, so watch the sentence figure: if you have 300 words in only three sentences, break them up. Use the paragraph count to make sure no block of text is overwhelming on mobile screens, where long paragraphs feel even longer. Finally, check the no-spaces character count when a form or database field limit applies to letters only.

A worked example

Take the sentence "Free online tools are fast." It has 5 words and 28 characters including spaces, or 23 without spaces. Each run of characters between spaces counts as one word, so a number like 2,500 or an address like name@example.com also counts as one.

Common uses

Students check essays against word limits. Freelancers invoice per word. SEO editors keep meta descriptions near 155 characters so search results do not cut them off.

Frequently asked questions

Does the word counter save my text?

No. Counting happens in your browser and the tool does not send your text to a server.

How is reading time calculated?

The estimate assumes an average adult reading speed of about 238 words per minute. Real reading speed varies with the material and the reader.

Are hyphenated words counted as one?

Yes. Any run of characters separated by spaces counts as one word, which matches how most word processors count.

Is there a length limit?

There is no fixed limit. Very large documents may feel slow on older phones.
